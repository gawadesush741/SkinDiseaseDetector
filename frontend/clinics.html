Urgent Red Flags: When to See a Doctor Promptly

If you notice any of the following signs, do not rely on AI screening. Schedule a medical examination immediately:

  • Spontaneous Bleeding or Oozing: A mole or spot that crusts, bleeds without trauma, or does not heal after 3 weeks.
  • Rapid Size or Shape Changes: Any lesion noticeably expanding over a period of weeks or months.
  • Multi-Colored Heterogeneity: Presence of uneven shades of black, dark brown, red, blue, or white within the same lesion.
  • Loss of Border Definition: Margins that are scalloped, ragged, notched, or fading indistinctly into normal skin.
  • New Sensory Symptoms: Persistent itching, pain, tenderness, or numbness around the spot.
  • The "Ugly Duckling" Sign: A mole that looks distinctly different from all other moles on your body.

What to Ask During Your Consultation

Take these clinical questions with your printed Dermalens summary to your appointment:

  1. "Does this lesion require dermoscopic evaluation or a diagnostic punch/shave biopsy?"
  2. "What features indicate whether this is a dysplastic nevus or a malignant lesion?"
  3. "How frequently should I undergo total-body photographic skin examinations?"
  4. "Given my skin type and family history, what is my baseline melanoma risk profile?"
